# Egypt food-service market: ability and willingness to pay for restaurant SaaS (2025-2026)

Research date: 2026-11-04. All figures are dated where the source dates them. Anything from a vendor blog, a paid-report summary or a search-engine snippet, and not from a primary source, is marked **UNVERIFIED**. No figure here was invented. Where no source was found, the item is listed under Gaps.

## 1. Size and structure of Egypt's food-service market, aggregators and commissions

### Takeaway
Egypt is a big, fragmented market dominated by independents. Paid-report summaries put it at about USD 12.4 bn in 2025, with about 71% of value going to independents. Nobody has published a reliable count of outlets: the only "400,000 establishments" figure comes from a vendor's own blog. Talabat is the leading delivery app. Its Egypt business grew about 50% a year in 2025. Commission rates are commonly said to be 15-25%, but that figure appears only in secondary blogs, never in published merchant terms.

### Cited Findings
**Market size and structure**

- Independent outlets held 70.88% market share in 2025 (Mordor Intelligence summary, **UNVERIFIED**). [Mordor via MarketResearch.com](https://marketresearch.com/Mordor-Intelligence-LLP-v4018/Egypt-Foodservice-Share-Trends-Statistics-36208639)

- QSR outlets grew from 17,626 in 2020 to 18,324 in 2022. Café/bar establishment count rose 1.4% from 2020 to 2021. - Elmenus: Walid El-Saadany became CEO in July 2025, succeeding founder Amir Allam. Its revenue model is a commission per order; no rate is published. [MENAbytes](https://www.menabytes.com/elmenus-founder-steps-down/)

**Commissions**
- Talabat is said to charge restaurants 15-25% of the order bill, with Egypt and Jordan at the lower end of the regional range. Exclusivity reportedly cuts the rate by 5-10 points. These figures come from undated third-party blogs that repeat each other (**UNVERIFIED**). [Menuviel blog](https://blog.menuviel.com/talabat-fees-and-commissions-for-restaurants/); [IdeaUsher](https://ideausher.com/blog/how-talabat-makes-money/)
- Enterprise (2021-10-13) also cites 15-25% for Talabat, attributing it to Oye Labs rather than to Talabat. In the same article, Enterprise's own office order paid a customer delivery fee of EGP 22 on an EGP 184 order. [Enterprise, 2021](https://enterpriseam.com/egypt/2021/10/13/are-restaurant-delivery-apps-a-modern-day-mafia-in-egypt-and-abroad/)
- Some regional blogs give a wider 15-35% range, depending on category, exclusivity and whether Talabat provides the delivery (**UNVERIFIED**). [Apptunix](https://apptunix.com/blog/talabat-business-model)

### Inferences
- At 15-25% commission, an Egyptian restaurant pays the aggregator EGP 150-250 for every EGP 1,000 of delivery orders. A restaurant doing EGP 100k/month through Talabat pays about EGP 15-25k/month in commission. That is roughly 5-10x the cost of a typical POS subscription (section 2). This supports pricing the ordering/QR tier as a commission-free direct channel and anchoring the value message on commission saved.
- Independents hold about 71% of value, so the buyer is mostly a single-outlet owner-operator. Tiers should be priced per branch, with a low-friction entry tier.
- The "400k establishments" figure is probably all food outlets including informal ones (ful/koshary stalls, ahwas). Treat it as the top of the funnel, not as the addressable SaaS market.

### Gaps
- No CAPMAS or official count of restaurants, cafés, cloud kitchens or food trucks was found for 2024-2026.
- No published Talabat, Elmenus, Breadfast, Mrsool or InstaShop merchant terms for Egypt. Commission rates are secondary-source only. Breadfast and InstaShop are mainly grocery and quick commerce, and no restaurant commission data was found for them. Mrsool is mainly Saudi and no Egypt data was found.
- No food-truck licensing rule or truck count was found.
- No figure for delivery-app penetration (share of restaurants listed, or share of food spend via apps) for Egypt was found.
- No Egypt-level GMV from Talabat filings was extracted; the IR deck linked above may contain it.

## 2. What Egyptian SMB venues spend on software/POS, how they pay, and local EGP price points

### Takeaway
Foodics, the regional leader, publishes Egypt bundles at about EGP 2,850-3,960 per branch per month (roughly USD 55-76). Egyptian low-cost vendors advertise from about EGP 149-500. The freelance market (Khamsat) sells one-time QR menus and cashier systems for USD 15-100, and some of these are pitched explicitly as "no monthly subscription". A credible ladder therefore runs from about EGP 150-500/month at the bottom to about EGP 3,000-4,000 at the full-POS top. Price sensitivity is high, and many buyers prefer to own outright rather than subscribe.

### Inferences
- At the 2026-10-01 CBE rate of about EGP 52.3/USD (section 3), Foodics Egypt Basic at EGP 2,849 is about USD 54 and Advanced at EGP 3,734 is about USD 71. That is roughly 45-50% of Foodics' Gulf USD price for the same tier (section 5). Foodics prices Egypt in EGP at a steep purchasing-power discount, not at USD parity.
- There is a clear price gap between about EGP 150-500/month (local entry vendors, unverified) and about EGP 2,850/month (Foodics Basic). An Egyptian POS tier at about EGP 800-1,800/month would sit below the market leader while staying well above the "cheap local" band. This is an inference from these data points, not a finding.
- The freelance "own it, no subscription" pitch (USD 20-100 one-time) means a QR-menu-only tier competes against near-zero one-time prices. It needs either a very low monthly price (about EGP 100-300 by inference) or a clear hosted, always-updated advantage.
- Foodics charges extra for "Digital Receipts". Compliance with the ETA e-receipt (section 4) is a monetisable add-on in this market.

### Gaps

- No data was found on how they pay SaaS vendors: share using card, InstaPay, Fawry, wallet or cash-to-agent. InstaPay merchant/B2B use for subscriptions was not covered by any source found.
- No Egyptian QR-menu SaaS with a published EGP subscription price was found. Social-media posts (Facebook, Instagram) with EGP prices were not reachable by search.
- No Egyptian hardware prices (touch POS terminal, thermal printer) were found in EGP.

## 3. Macroeconomic context: EGP/USD, inflation, foreign SaaS pricing, VAT

### Takeaway
The pound was devalued from about 30.85 to about 50 per USD on 2024-03-06. It traded at about 47.7 at the start of 2026 and weakened to about 52.3 by 2026-10-01. Inflation has come down from a 2023 peak of 38-40% to roughly 12.8-14.5% in August-September 2026, and the CBE expected it to rise further in Q3 2026. VAT is 14% on SaaS. Prices for any EGP list should be reviewed at least every 6-12 months.

### Inferences
- From 2023 to 2026 the USD price of an EGP-denominated subscription fell by about 40% (30.85 to about 52 EGP/USD), while local costs rose with about 13-38% annual inflation. A vendor that set EGP prices in 2023 and never raised them has cut its real price sharply. Pricing should include a stated annual review or an inflation-linked uplift clause.
- Annual prepay in EGP is unusually attractive to buyers when inflation is in the teens, because it locks in today's price. It is also costly to the vendor for the same reason. Discounts for annual billing should therefore be modest (about 1-2 months free, in line with Cloud Markit's "saves two months"), not deep.
- An Egyptian B2B buyer is a VAT-registered business, so quoting "excluding 14% VAT" with VAT shown at checkout matches the Gulf practice. Small venues that are not VAT-registered cannot reclaim it, so for them the VAT-inclusive price is the real price. This is an inference; Egyptian convention was not confirmed.

### Gaps
- No primary CBE or CAPMAS page was fetched; the rates above come from news reports of CBE and CAPMAS data.
- The outcome of the 2026-10-29 MPC meeting and the November 2026 exchange rate were not found.
- No confirmed list of foreign SaaS vendors' Egypt-specific EGP price points was found beyond Foodics (for example Odoo, Loyverse, Square-equivalents).
- No source settled whether Egyptian SaaS sellers usually quote VAT-inclusive prices.

## 4. ETA e-receipt (B2C) mandate

### Takeaway
The e-receipt system is rolled out in named-taxpayer phases set by ETA decisions, not by a sector-wide or turnover-based deadline. Recent waves were Decision 281/2025 (effective 2025-09-15) and Decision 361/2025 (ninth phase, first sub-phase, effective 2025-11-15). No source named restaurants or cafés as a category for 2026. Any venue on an ETA list must issue e-receipts through an integrated POS. That makes e-receipt integration a POS buying criterion, and Foodics already sells it as an add-on.

### Cited Findings
- Decision No. 361 of 2025 covers the first sub-phase of the ninth main phase. It obliges taxpayers on an attached list to issue e-receipts on the live system for sales to final consumers from 2025-11-15. The list is published on the ETA site. [Invoice Data Extraction guide](https://invoicedataextraction.com/blog/egypt-electronic-receipt-compliance-guide); Arabic news coverage: [Al Bawaba News](https://www.albawabhnews.com/5268187)
- Decision No. 281 of 2025 extended B2C e-receipts to more taxpayers (eighth sub-phase of the second stage), effective 2025-09-15. [RTC Suite](https://rtcsuite.com/?p=11585); [Comarch](https://abc.comarch.com/trade-and-services/data-management/legal-regulation-changes/egypt-expands-e-receipt-requirements-for-b2c-transactions-from-september-2025)
- A "sixth wave" came in January 2025. Earlier, Decision No. 139 of 2024 covered the fourth sub-phase of the fourth phase. [ClearTax](https://cleartax.com/eg/en/e-invoicing-egypt); [Bnok24](https://www.bnok24.com/?p=153144)
- Scope is set by named lists, not a universal turnover threshold. One guide says there is no support for claims that a universal threshold changed on 2026-01-01. [Invoice Data Extraction guide](https://invoicedataextraction.com/blog/egypt-electronic-receipt-compliance-guide)
- Sources conflict on when B2C e-receipts started: a pilot in April 2022, or July 2023. [VATupdate](https://www.vatupdate.com/?p=339313); [ClearTax](https://cleartax.com/eg/en/e-invoicing-egypt); [Ecosire](https://ecosire.com/tr/blog/odoo-egypt-localization-guide-2026)
- Official ETA lookups: e-receipt services, decisions and lists at [eta.gov.eg e-receipt services](https://www.eta.gov.eg/ar/content/e-receipt-services); per-taxpayer coverage check at [eta.gov.eg e-receipt inquiry](https://www.eta.gov.eg/ar/ereceipt-inquiry). These pages were not fetched.
- Foodics lists "Digital Receipts" as an add-on on its Egypt Basic and Advanced plans, and its blog says modern POS systems can issue e-invoices that meet ETA requirements. [Foodics Egypt pricing](https://www.foodics.com/foodics-pricing-egypt/); [Foodics blog](https://www.foodics.com/cashier-system-restaurants-role-in-egypt-2/)

### Inferences
- A venue that has been named in an ETA phase cannot keep using a non-integrated cashier, so an ETA-integrated POS becomes a must-have for it. That supports putting e-receipt in the paid POS tier or selling it as a separately priced add-on, as Foodics does.
- Because coverage is list-based, many small cafés, trucks and informal venues are not yet required to comply. For them e-receipt is not a reason to buy today. Pricing should not force it into the entry tier.

### Gaps
